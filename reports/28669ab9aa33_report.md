## 12. Synthesis

The current research on red OLED photobiomodulation is at an early but promising stage. Preclinical studies, primarily in hair growth and wound healing models, show positive biological effects attributed to mitochondrial stimulation, consistent with the broader PBM literature. The unique properties of OLEDs—flexibility, low heat, and potential for wearable patches—represent a significant advantage for home-based or continuous therapy. However, the field faces several key challenges: limited human evidence, lower irradiance compared to LEDs/lasers, and a lack of standardized treatment parameters. The mechanistic basis is well-supported from general PBM research, but OLED-specific studies are needed to confirm that the light output is sufficient to trigger these pathways in humans. The most defensible conclusion is that red OLED PBM is a promising technology with strong preclinical rationale, but its clinical efficacy remains unproven, and further high-quality human trials are essential to determine its place among phototherapy modalities.
